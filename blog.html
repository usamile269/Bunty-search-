How to Protect Your Personal Information Online

Guide · September 11, 2026

Personal information can travel quickly once it is posted online. Before sharing a phone number, identity document, address, or screenshot, ask whether the recipient genuinely needs it and whether the channel is trustworthy.

Use unique passwords, enable two-factor authentication, review app permissions, and avoid sending one-time codes to anyone. Regularly search your own public profiles and remove information you no longer want available.

How to Handle Unknown Calls Safely

Safety guide · September 11, 2026

An unknown caller may be harmless, but urgent requests for money, verification codes, or account access are warning signs. Do not share an OTP or click a link during an unexpected call.

End the call, verify the organisation through its official website, block repeated spam, and report fraud to the relevant provider or authorities. Save evidence without forwarding it to others.

Responsible Use of Search Tools

Best practices · September 11, 2026

Search tools should help people find information they are allowed to access, not expose private data or enable harassment. Only search information with a legitimate reason and follow applicable laws and service rules.

Do not publish another person's phone number, identity information, address, or search result without permission. If you find incorrect or harmful information, contact the site administrator and request a correction or removal.

Strong Passwords and Safer Accounts

Security guide · September 11, 2026

A strong password is long, unique, and not reused across important accounts. A password manager can create and store different passwords so one data breach does not expose every account.

Turn on two-factor authentication, keep recovery details current, and review active sessions. Never share a password, recovery code, or OTP—even if a message appears to come from support.

How to Check Whether Online Information Is Reliable

Digital literacy · September 11, 2026

Do not rely on a single result. Compare the claim with official sources, check the publication date, look for the original context, and distinguish an opinion from a documented fact.

Be careful with screenshots and forwarded messages because they can be edited or taken out of context. When information affects money, safety, or identity, verify it directly with the responsible organisation.

Privacy Checklist for Everyday Browsing

Checklist · September 11, 2026
  • Use HTTPS and keep your browser updated.
  • Review location, camera, microphone, and contact permissions.
  • Remove unused apps and browser extensions.
  • Use privacy controls for advertising and cookies.
  • Think carefully before posting permanent personal details.

Small, consistent habits reduce risk more effectively than waiting until an account is compromised.
